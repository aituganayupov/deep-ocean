Zone 1 · 0 – 200 m

The Sunlight Zone

This is the top layer of the ocean. It is full of light and the water is warm. Tiny plants called phytoplankton grow here, and they feed many animals, so most sea life lives in this zone.

?

Did you know?

Phytoplankton make about half of the oxygen on Earth. So every second breath you take comes from the ocean!

?

Did you know?

Dolphins sleep with only half of their brain at a time. The other half stays awake, so they can breathe and watch for danger.

1,000m
Zone 2 · 200 – 1,000 m

The Twilight Zone

Here the light is very weak, like the sky after sunset. Plants can't grow, because there isn't enough sunlight. Many animals have huge eyes to see in the dark, and some can make their own light.

?

Did you know?

Every night, billions of animals swim up from the Twilight Zone to eat near the surface. In the morning they swim back down. It is the biggest daily trip of animals on Earth!

?

Did you know?

Jellyfish have no brain, no heart and no bones. Their body is about 95% water.

Zone 3 · 1,000 – 4,000 m

The Midnight Zone

No sunlight ever reaches this zone. The water is black and cold, only about 4 °C. The only light here comes from animals. This light is called bioluminescence. Move your mouse to make the water glow!

?

Did you know?

Scientists think that about three out of four deep-sea animals can make their own light.

?

Did you know?

Sperm whales dive down to more than 2,000 m to hunt squid. They can hold their breath for about 90 minutes.

The Abyss

The Abyss is a dark, cold world near the ocean floor. The water is almost freezing, and the pressure is 400 to 600 times stronger than at the surface. Even so, some very strange animals live here.

The flat floor of the deep ocean covers about half of Earth's surface. That is more than all the land on our planet!

Zone 5 · 6,000 – 10,935 m

The Trench

Welcome to the deepest place on Earth! The Challenger Deep in the Mariana Trench, in the Pacific Ocean, is about 11,000 m deep. The pressure here is more than 1,000 times stronger than at the surface, but there is still life.

Could Everest fit inside?

Yes, easily! Mount Everest is 8,849 m tall. If you put it at the bottom of the Challenger Deep, its top would still be about 2,000 m under water. The drawing is to scale.

?

Did you know?

In 1960, Jacques Piccard and Don Walsh went to the bottom in a submarine called Trieste. It took them almost 5 hours to go down!

?

Did you know?

In 2012, the film director James Cameron went down to the Challenger Deep alone, in a small green submarine.
